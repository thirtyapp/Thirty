# THIRTY Playbook
## Chapter 2 — Circle Experience System

*This chapter builds directly on [Chapter 1 — The Circle Manifesto](01-the-circle-manifesto.md). It does not restate that philosophy; it translates it into how THIRTY must feel, moment to moment. Where this chapter is silent, Chapter 1 governs.*

---

## 1. Introduction

An interface is what a person looks at. An experience is what a person carries with them after they've stopped looking. Most products are designed and judged at the level of the interface — does the button work, is the layout clear, does the screen load. THIRTY must be designed and judged one level deeper: not "does this screen work," but "what does a person feel three seconds after they close it."

This distinction is not academic. Two products can share an identical interface — same layout, same colors, same copy — and produce opposite experiences, because experience lives in timing, restraint, and what was left out. A progress ring and a countdown timer can be visually the same circle and psychologically opposite objects: one says *you are becoming whole*, the other says *you are running out*.

This chapter exists because THIRTY's interfaces will change — screens get redesigned, components get rebuilt, platforms get added. The experience must not change with them. A designer five years from now, working in a design language that doesn't exist yet, should still be able to read this chapter and know exactly what a new screen is supposed to make someone feel. That durability is the point: this document outlives every interface built from it.

## 2. The Daily Emotional Journey

THIRTY is not a sequence of screens. It is a sequence of feelings, and the screens exist to carry them. Each stage below describes the feeling that stage must produce — not the interface that produces it.

**New Day.** A quiet sense of possibility, without pressure. Nothing has been asked of the user yet, and nothing they did or didn't do yesterday follows them into today. The feeling is closer to the first sip of a morning drink than to an alert demanding attention.

**Ready.** A settled, unhurried readiness — the feeling of standing at the top of a staircase you already know, not the edge of a cliff. There is no anxiety about what's ahead, because nothing ahead is unknown or unreasonable.

**Start.** A small, deliberate threshold-crossing. The feeling of a decision made, not a task launched. It should feel closer to stepping onto a path than to pressing "play" — quieter, more personal, less transactional.

**Active.** Focused presence. The user's attention should be on the activity, not on the product. If a person is thinking about THIRTY while they're inside a session, the experience has failed at this stage — the product's job here is to become invisible.

**Almost Complete.** Anticipation without urgency. A sense that something is nearly whole, felt the way a person feels watching the last few minutes of a good, familiar film — not rushing toward the end, simply aware it's coming.

**Circle Completed.** Quiet satisfaction. Not triumph, not celebration — the feeling of setting something down gently because it's finished, not because it's finally over. This is the single most important feeling THIRTY produces, and the one most vulnerable to being overplayed (see §5, §6, §10).

**Reflection.** Calm, low-stakes noticing. The feeling of glancing back at something without being asked to account for it — closer to a passing thought than a review.

**Closure.** Contentment that doesn't need to be reinforced. The user should feel finished with THIRTY the way they feel finished with a good meal: satisfied, not eager for more, not wondering if they should check back later.

## 3. The Circle Lifecycle

Every Circle passes through the same states, regardless of what fills it. Each state has a purpose, a visual meaning, an emotional meaning, and a transition into the next. These are described as meanings to preserve, not surfaces to build — how they are rendered is a design decision made elsewhere, bound by what is written here.

**Open.** *Purpose:* to represent a day not yet lived. *Visual meaning:* emptiness that reads as potential, not deficiency — an empty circle is a beginning, never a shortfall. *Emotional meaning:* invitation. *Transition:* opens automatically, without ceremony, at the start of each day — the least dramatic moment in the entire lifecycle, because it needs no drama to matter.

**Filling.** *Purpose:* to represent effort already given. *Visual meaning:* growth without a ceiling of judgment — the shape communicates "more than before," never "not enough yet." *Emotional meaning:* momentum, ownership. *Transition:* accumulates smoothly and only in response to real, user-driven progress — it never fills on its own, and it never fills faster than the effort behind it.

**Nearly Closed.** *Purpose:* to mark the final stretch without manufacturing urgency. *Visual meaning:* wholeness approaching, not a deadline approaching. *Emotional meaning:* anticipation, not pressure. *Transition:* moves into completion the moment the last real effort lands — never early, never with an artificial delay added purely for suspense.

**Closed.** *Purpose:* to mark that today's ritual is complete. *Visual meaning:* wholeness — a shape with nothing missing. *Emotional meaning:* the satisfaction described in §2. *Transition:* holds, briefly and without demand, before settling into the archive — this is the one moment in the lifecycle allowed to ask for a beat of stillness before moving on.

**Archived.** *Purpose:* to record that a day happened, without scoring it against any other day. *Visual meaning:* a completed shape among other completed shapes — no ranking, no chain, no comparison. *Emotional meaning:* quiet permanence. A closed circle from three weeks ago must feel exactly as valid as one closed this morning. *Transition:* it does not transition again. It simply remains true.

**Unopened** (a day with no circle at all) is not a state of failure and is not styled as one. It is the visual absence of a circle, nothing more — no broken shape, no red marker, no chain to show broken. Chapter 1 §7 forbids the streak; this is where that rule becomes a lifecycle state instead of an abstract promise. An unopened day simply is not there, the way yesterday's weather isn't there. It is not remembered as a debt.

## 4. Motion Principles

- **Motion must mean something before it may exist.** If a movement cannot be explained by what it communicates, it is decoration, and decoration is not permitted to move on its own.
- **Nothing moves without the user, or without a reason tied to their action.** Ambient, idle, or "alive" motion — animation that plays simply to make a screen feel dynamic — has no place here. Stillness is not an unfinished state; it is frequently the correct one.
- **Transitions should lower the user's heart rate, not raise it.** A transition's job is to make a change feel expected and safe, never to add excitement the moment didn't need. If a transition makes a screen feel more urgent than the action that triggered it, the transition is wrong, not the action.
- **Movement should reduce the effort of understanding, never add to it.** Motion exists to show a user where something came from and where it's going, so they don't have to reconstruct that relationship themselves. If a user has to think about an animation, it has already failed at its one job.
- **Speed communicates weight.** A small change may move quickly; something significant — the closing of a circle, above all — is allowed to take a breath. Duration is not specified here in milliseconds because the correct duration is not a number, it is a feeling: does this movement feel rushed, or does it feel considered. When those two answers disagree with a stopwatch, trust the feeling.
- **Restraint scales with importance.** Counterintuitively, the most significant moment in the product — the Circle closing — earns the least spectacle, not the most (Chapter 1 §12). The bigger the moment, the more motion must get out of its way.

## 5. Haptic Principles

A haptic is a physical promise: it tells the body something is true before the mind has read a word. Because it bypasses language, it is powerful, and because it is powerful, it must be rare. A product that uses haptics often teaches the body to ignore them — the exact opposite of what a physical confirmation is for.

THIRTY uses haptic feedback at exactly two moments, and no others by default:

- **Starting a Circle.** A single, soft confirmation that a decision has been made — the physical equivalent of a door closing quietly behind you as you step through it.
- **Completing a Circle.** The most important haptic in the entire product (Chapter 1 §12). Soft, brief, singular — felt, not announced. It confirms without congratulating.

Haptics are never used for: marketing messages, upsell prompts, streak or count milestones, re-engagement nudges, or any notification designed to create urgency or anxiety. The reasoning is the same in every case — a haptic used to sell something or to provoke a reaction teaches the user's body to associate that physical sensation with being managed, not accompanied. Once that association forms, it cannot be bought back by using the haptic more gently later. Scarcity is what gives it meaning; using it for anything but the two moments above spends that scarcity for a purpose smaller than what it cost.

## 6. Audio Principles

Silence is not the absence of an audio design. It is the default of one. Before any sound is added to a moment, the correct question is not "what should this sound like" but "does this moment actually need sound, or does it only need to not be interrupted." Most moments in THIRTY answer that question with silence.

- **Silence is preferable** during Open, Filling, Nearly Closed, Reflection, and Closure — the majority of the daily journey. These are moments of attention, not moments that need scoring.
- **Circle Sessions** may offer optional ambient sound — a guided breath, a soft rhythm, an audio companion to the activity itself. This is opt-in, never autoplay, and never louder in intention than the activity it accompanies. Its purpose is to support focus, not to make the product feel more alive.
- **The completion tone**, where used, is the one sound the product is allowed to treat as meaningful: single, soft, and brief — matching the restraint of the haptic it accompanies at the same moment (§5). It confirms; it does not perform.
- Premium deepens audio the same way it deepens everything else (§9): a richer Circle Session might offer a more considered ambient layer or a guided voice, but the underlying rule does not change. More depth is not permission for more volume, more frequency, or more insistence. A Premium user should notice the sound is more thoughtful, never that there is simply more of it.

## 7. Interaction Principles

- **One primary action, always.** Every screen answers one question: what is the one thing a person is here to do. If a screen offers two actions of equal visual and cognitive weight, it has not finished being designed — one of them is not actually primary, and pretending otherwise asks the user to do the product's job of deciding.
- **No confirmation for what confirms itself.** A confirmation dialog is appropriate only when an action is destructive or irreversible. Asking a user to confirm something they can plainly see and easily undo does not add safety — it adds friction, and friction is not a substitute for care.
- **No hidden complexity.** Nothing about how THIRTY works should require the user to discover it by accident or be told about it out-of-band. If a feature needs a tutorial to be understood, the feature is more complicated than the ritual can carry, not merely under-explained.
- **Every interaction must increase clarity, not just enable an action.** An interaction that lets a user do something while leaving them unsure what just happened, or what happens next, has technically worked and experientially failed.

## 8. Reflection Principles

Reflection in THIRTY is an offer, extended once, that the user is free to decline without consequence. It is not a form to complete, not a streak of check-ins to maintain, and not a mechanism the product uses to extract more data than the activity itself already provided.

- **Reflection is invited, never required.** No circle depends on it closing. No feature is withheld from a user who never reflects on anything.
- **Reflection never carries judgment.** There is no "correct" reflection, no scored response, no comparison to a prior day's answer implying one was better.
- **Reflection is never used as a punishment or a gate.** It is not asked of a user only on days they underperformed, and it is never framed as something owed after a missed day. If reflection only appears when something went wrong, it has quietly become a penalty wearing a gentler name.
- Its purpose is entirely for the user, in the moment it's offered. If a reflection response is ever repurposed to justify a product decision the user wouldn't recognize as its origin, that reuse violates this principle regardless of how it's implemented downstream.

## 9. Premium Experience

Premium exists to make the ritual feel more understood, not to make the free experience feel more incomplete (Chapter 1 §9). Each Premium concept below deepens a part of the Circle that already exists — none of them introduce a new object the free experience was missing.

- **Circle Sessions.** A guided version of the daily thirty minutes — more considered structure, optional ambient audio (§6), a more deliberate pace. The philosophy: the free Circle is never unguided in a way that feels incomplete; a Session simply offers a more accompanied way to fill the same circle that was always whole on its own.
- **Circle Insights.** A deeper look at the pattern behind the circles a user has already closed. The philosophy: insight is a mirror, not a report card. It reflects what's true back to the user with more nuance — it never introduces scores, rankings, or comparisons that weren't present in the free experience's quiet counting of closed circles.
- **Circle Coach.** A more personal layer of guidance from the same AI companion every user already has. The philosophy: the free companion is never deliberately withheld intelligence to create a gap — Coach is a closer, more contextual version of a relationship that already existed, not the unlocking of a relationship that was previously absent.
- **Circle Plans.** A longer arc connecting individual circles into a considered shape over weeks, for users who want that shape. The philosophy: a Plan organizes circles the user was always going to close anyway; it never requires a Plan for a single day's circle to feel complete and worthwhile on its own.

The standing test, repeated from Chapter 1 §9 because it is the one that matters most here: if a Premium concept can only be explained by contrasting it with something free users are missing, it has failed, however well it is built.

## 10. Experience Rules

1. Everything reinforces the Circle. Nothing competes with it for meaning.
2. One meaningful action per screen — never two things asking for attention at once.
3. Silence is a feature, not an empty space waiting to be filled.
4. Completion is the reward. It requires no additional reward layered on top.
5. Never punish absence. A day without a circle is empty, not broken.
6. Motion communicates intention — if it doesn't, it doesn't move.
7. Haptics are physical trust. Spend them rarely, or they stop meaning anything.
8. Premium deepens. It never gates what the ritual already promised for free.
9. Reflection never judges. An invitation that can quietly become an obligation is no longer an invitation.
10. The next Circle always begins with hope, regardless of what happened to the last one.
11. Restraint is not the absence of design — at the moments that matter most, it is the design.
12. Clarity outranks cleverness in every interaction, without exception.
13. Consistency is a form of respect for the user's attention; novelty for its own sake is not.
14. Nothing in THIRTY should require the user to feel bad in order to feel motivated.
15. A feature that increases engagement but decreases calm has failed, regardless of its metrics.
16. The user's context and intent outrank the product's preference for what would be impressive to show.
17. What THIRTY doesn't say is as deliberate as what it does say.
18. Data about the user is used to understand them, never to score, rank, or diagnose them.
19. Every new feature must survive contact with the Golden Rule before it survives contact with a roadmap.
20. If a decision cannot be explained simply, it is not ready to ship — complexity hidden in an explanation is still complexity.

## 11. Experience Checklist

Before any feature, screen, or flow ships, it must be able to answer these honestly:

- Does this strengthen the Circle, or does it compete with it for meaning?
- Does this reduce the user's mental effort, or does it add a decision they didn't need to make?
- Does this introduce noise — visual, motion, sound, or notification — that wasn't earned by the moment?
- Would this still make sense if every trend driving it today no longer existed?
- Does this create calm, or does it create a reason to come back that has nothing to do with the ritual itself?
- If this were removed entirely, would the user lose something real, or just something the team was proud of building?
- Does this treat a missed day, a skipped session, or an ignored suggestion as information, or as a failure?
- Is this here because it serves the user's thirty minutes, or because it serves a metric about the user's thirty minutes?
- Could this be explained to a new user in one calm sentence?
- Does this still feel true to a person having their worst week, not only their best one?

A feature that cannot answer these questions well is not ready — regardless of how complete it is technically.

## 12. Closing Statement

When a user closes a Circle, they should feel the way a person feels after closing a door quietly behind them at the end of a good, ordinary day: settled, a little lighter, and certain — without needing to check — that they did something that mattered. Not impressed with the product. Not eager to open it again. Simply at ease, having kept a small promise to themselves, with nothing left to prove until tomorrow's circle opens on its own.
