## Purpose of this document

While the V1 World artwork was being made and integrated, a commercial idea came up naturally. Some objects in the Worlds could one day exist as real things, and THIRTY's ritual could be carried into analog products that work away from the screen. [THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md) already records the *design* side of that idea: which props recur in the art, how stable they are, and what rules would protect the Worlds. This document records the *commercial* side:

- the principle a THIRTY physical product would have to meet;
- which Signature Objects look commercially interesting, and why;
- the Garden Window `tend` utility basket / caddy as a deliberately chosen candidate;
- a THIRTY adult coloring ritual book as an explicit future opportunity;
- the difference between *merchandise* and *ritual products*;
- a lightweight filter and a low-risk validation sequence for later.

| **[FUTURE POSSIBILITY]** | An idea that might become relevant much later. Not scoped. |

No cost, margin, price, volume, supplier quote or revenue figure appears anywhere in this document. Operational complexity is described only as LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH, as a qualitative first impression to be replaced by real data.

The repository had no `docs/strategy/` folder before this document. It was chosen deliberately:

- [`docs/product/`](../product/README.md) holds the approved Premium strategy, the Premium freeze and ADRs. A commercial document placed there could be misread as product scope.

## 1. Strategic principle

> **A THIRTY physical product should feel as if it came out of a THIRTY World — not like generic merchandise with a THIRTY logo printed on it.**

This extends the test already recorded in [Signature Objects §9.1](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md#91-principles) to every possible physical product, not only objects taken from the artwork.

Recognisability should come primarily from:

- **object silhouette**, the outline someone recognises at a glance;
- **materials**, such as cotton knit, woven fibre, stoneware, paper and felted rubber;
- **texture**, such as knit, weave, watercolor grain and uncoated paper;
- **palette**, meaning Circle Sage, Mist Sage, Warm Stone and Cream plus the owning World's single approved pigment ([World System §10a](../worlds/WORLD_SYSTEM.md#10a-shared-world-art-rules-v1));
- **details**, such as a fringe, a leather tab or a fold;
- **ritual use**, meaning what someone actually does with it during their thirty minutes;
- **relation to a World**, meaning a recognisable Place behind it.

Branding can be subtle, at most a small tonal or blind-embossed mark, as [Signature Objects §5](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md#5-object-identity-contract--proposed-rule) already proposes. **A large logo is never the main differentiator.** The Brand Book's "never gold / no status signals" instinct ([Playbook Ch.3 §12](../playbook/03-circle-design-language.md#12-premium-design)) applies.

### Why this may be strategically stronger [HYPOTHESIS]

1. **Deeper World identity.** A product that carries a World's materials and palette may strengthen the World in the app. A logo product strengthens only the logo.
2. **Emotional continuity.** If the app's evening Garden Window and a real sage throw on a real chair share one feeling, the ritual continues off-screen. This is the emotional core of the opportunity.
3. **More premium feel.** Restraint, material and detail tend to read as more premium than prominent branding. This matches THIRTY's "calm, premium" positioning ([Brand Book §24](../BRAND_BOOK.md#24-future-brand-evolution)).
4. **Less generic creator merch.** Logo apparel and mugs are the default for app and creator brands. Products that don't need a logo are harder to copy and easier to tell apart.
5. **Potential ecosystem effect.** Several products from one World could feel like they belong together. *Collectible pressure* is explicitly excluded (§10 and [Signature Objects §13](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md#13-anti-gamification)). Any ecosystem effect must come from coherence, never from "complete the set".
6. **Stronger organic storytelling.** "This basket is from the Garden Window" is a story. "This mug has our logo on it" is not. Product stories may travel more naturally in social content and gifting.

**[VALIDATION QUESTION]** Do people outside the app recognise and value a product's World origin, or does the story only work for existing users? (§2, "Stands alone without the app".)

### 2.1 Assessment legend

Each object below is assessed on the same dimensions. Complexity terms are qualitative first impressions:

- **Manufacture:** how hard it looks to make well at small scale.
- **Shipping:** how practical it looks to pack and send (size, weight, shape).
- **Fragility:** how likely it is to arrive damaged.
- **Return risk:** how likely returns look (sizing, colour mismatch, damage, subjective feel).
- **Margin character:** a qualitative note on what tends to drive margin in the category. Never a number.
- **Differentiation:** how much THIRTY-specific identity the object can carry without a logo.
- **Stands alone without the app:** whether someone who has never seen THIRTY would still want it.

## 3. Garden Window `tend` — utility basket / caddy

This object was **deliberately chosen** as the most commercially interesting Signature Object candidate. This section records why. Everything below is **[HYPOTHESIS]**.

### 3.1 Why the basket, not the watering can

The watering can is the more obvious "tending" symbol. The soft woven utility basket / caddy looks commercially stronger because it is:

- **broader in utility**: almost every home has something to gather, carry or store, while far fewer people water plants regularly;
- **less niche**: it isn't tied to gardening or to owning plants;
- **easier to fit into different rituals**: tending, reading, writing, listening and comfort all involve a few small objects that need a home;
- **potentially easier to ship**: a soft or flexible woven form may pack flatter and survive transport better than a metal can, though this depends on construction (**[VALIDATION QUESTION]**);
- **suitable as a container for future bundles**: it can *hold* other THIRTY products, so it is a product and packaging at once;
- **visually recognisable**: the combination of a sage-toned weave and tan leather tabs is specific, and it is already the focal object of all three `tend` Cards [OBSERVED];
- **capable of becoming a recurring THIRTY object**: the category could reasonably appear in other Worlds where it belongs to the Place, without forcing it. World-exclusive motifs and the "never inserted for merchandise" rule still apply ([SO §2, §11](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md#11-world-system-compatibility)).

### 3.2 Possible future uses [FUTURE POSSIBILITY]

- a **home reset / tending caddy**: cloth, small tools and a few things to put back in their place;
- **reading or ritual storage** beside a chair;
- a container for a **blanket, notebook or headphones**: the objects of the Reading Nook kept in one place;
- a **gift bundle container**, where the basket is the gift and also holds the gift.

### 3.3 Boundaries

- **[NON-COMMITMENT]** No SKU plan, size range, colourway set, material specification or price is created here.
- The basket must stay a *tending* object in its World. It must never become a shopping basket, a "cart" metaphor or a product display inside the app.
- **[VALIDATION QUESTION]** Can a small producer make the sage weave plus leather-tab combination well, consistently and in small quantities? Is the leather detail compatible with the materials people expect from THIRTY (for example, would a non-animal alternative be preferred)?

## 4. THIRTY adult coloring ritual book

This opportunity is **important** and is recorded explicitly.

### 4.1 Framing

It is **not**: *"THIRTY sells a generic mandala coloring book."*

It **is** **[OPPORTUNITY]**: *a THIRTY adult coloring ritual product, built around quiet, finite sessions, with visual language drawn from the THIRTY Worlds.*

The core concept is **"30 quiet minutes"**: a contained coloring ritual with a clear start and a natural end. It mirrors the Circle: one bounded session that is complete in itself, not an endless task. A page or spread is sized to be a satisfying session, not a project that is never finished.

"Mandala" is used here as a shorthand for the *genre*: symmetrical, contemplative, repeating patterns. The product should keep the genre's calm and symmetry without inheriting its commoditised look (§5.2).

### 4.2 World-derived visual vocabulary [OPPORTUNITY]

Each World provides an **inspiration system**, a vocabulary of forms. These are **not** screenshots or artwork converted into line drawings.

| World | Possible vocabulary |
|---|---|
| **Quiet Trail** | Leaves, winding pathways, the rhythm of grasses and wildflowers, forest and hill forms, layered horizons |
| **Still Lake** | Water ripples, concentric rings, reeds and cattails, rounded stones, calm horizontal symmetry: the most naturally "mandala-like" World |
| **Open Room** | Soft geometry, movement lines, textile and mat patterns, window grids, pools of light on floorboards, open spatial forms |
| **Reading Nook** | Textile weaves and fringes, book and page forms, cushion patterns, lamp-shade geometry, trailing ivy, decorative motifs |
| **Garden Window** | Botanicals, leaves, window-pane geometry, basket weaves, garden beds, small domestic still lifes |

**[HYPOTHESIS]** Following the spirit of World-exclusive motifs ([§10a](../worlds/WORLD_SYSTEM.md#10a-shared-world-art-rules-v1)), each World's vocabulary could stay within its own chapter or series. This would keep the Worlds distinguishable on paper as they are on screen. §10a governs artwork, not printed products, so this is only a design suggestion.

The book should **work for adults** and **avoid feeling childish**. That means fine but not exhausting line work, mature composition, generous margins, and the hand-felt line that [Illustration Language](../illustration/ILLUSTRATION_LANGUAGE.md) asks for ("a line with natural variation reads as made by someone").

### 4.3 Positioning directions [OPPORTUNITY]

None is selected.

- **coloring ritual**: a ritual first, a book second;
- **quiet pages**: the calm of the page, not the volume of content;
- **slow coloring**: unhurried, finite, without pressure to finish everything;
- **visual reset**: a short break that leaves you calmer ([Brand Promise](../BRAND_BOOK.md#5-brand-promise));
- **analog THIRTY ritual**: the thirty minutes, away from the screen.

### 4.4 Naming directions [OPPORTUNITY]

These are exploration only. **No name is selected, checked or approved.**

- *Thirty Quiet Pages*
- *THIRTY Worlds — A Coloring Ritual*
- *30 Minutes of Color*
- *The THIRTY Coloring Ritual*
- *Quiet Worlds by THIRTY*

**[VALIDATION QUESTION]** Trademark and marketplace availability; spelling for the market (UK "colour" vs US "color"); and whether "30 minutes" in a name implies a timing promise the product should not make.

## 5. Why the coloring product may be commercially interesting

### 5.1 Potential advantages to investigate [HYPOTHESIS]

**None of these is proven economics.** Each must be checked against real quotes, samples and test results.

- **Relatively simple product.** Paper and binding, with no hardware, textiles or sizing.
- **Potentially low fulfilment complexity.** Flat, light and stackable.
- **Inexpensive first prototypes.** Sample pages can be tested before any print run.
- **Print-on-demand may allow validation without inventory.** Quality must be checked, because POD paper and binding may not meet THIRTY's bar (§5.2).
- **Natural link to the 30-minute ritual.** The concept and the slogan *"Your healthiest 30 minutes."* already fit together.
- **Accessible gift.** A low-commitment way to give someone "THIRTY" without them installing anything.
- **No sizing, no charging, no electronics.**
- **Low breakage risk.**
- **International shipping may be simpler** than for bulky or fragile goods. Duties, VAT and channel rules still apply.
- **Visual reach across all five Worlds.** It is the only product idea that gives Quiet Trail and Still Lake a natural expression.
- **Series potential.** For example, one volume per World or per season. Never sold through scarcity or "complete the set" pressure (§10).
- **Independent of the subscription.** It can exist without the app, Premium or any account.

### 5.2 Risks

- **Highly competitive market.** Adult coloring books are abundant and cheap.
- **Easy to look generic.** Without strong THIRTY DNA, it becomes one more pattern book.
- **"Mandala" can feel commoditised.** The genre is saturated with interchangeable symmetrical designs.
- **Poor art could cheapen the brand.** A weak physical product reflects on the app.
- **AI-looking patterns could harm premium perception.** Uniform, over-detailed or incoherent line work is easy to recognise and conflicts with Illustration Language's "felt hand".
- **Copyright and IP must be clear.** The rights to every page, and to any World artwork it derives from, must be owned or properly licensed for commercial print. **[VALIDATION QUESTION]** What is the rights position of the approved World artwork for commercial reproduction?
- **Paper and print quality matter.** Bleed-through, thin paper or weak binding ruin the ritual.
- **Not enough distinctive THIRTY DNA.** It needs the World vocabularies, not just a logo on the cover.
- **"Wellness cliché" positioning.** Stress-relief clichés and lotus imagery would misplace THIRTY. Claims also matter: the product must not make therapeutic, clinical or health-outcome claims. This is consistent with THIRTY's refusal of unsupported causal health claims (*Product Direction & Scope Delta*).

## 6. Product ecosystem: ritual products, not just merch

### 6.1 The distinction [HYPOTHESIS]

| | **Merchandise** | **Ritual products** |
|---|---|---|
| Primary job | Brand expression | Helping someone perform a THIRTY-like ritual, even outside the app |
| What carries it | Mostly the mark | Use, material, form and World origin |
| Value without the brand | Usually low | Should be high |
| Risk | Generic, cheap, logo-led | Over-designed, or seen as unnecessary |

**[HYPOTHESIS]** THIRTY may be commercially stronger if it leans toward **ritual products** rather than conventional merch. The product would then extend the promise ("leave the user calmer than before") into the physical world instead of just advertising the app.

### 6.2 Examples [OPPORTUNITY]

- a **notebook designed for Write**: quiet prompts or open pages, sized for one short session, with no streak tracking (§10);
- the **quiet coloring book** (§4);
- **physical ritual cards**: a small deck expressing THIRTY's kind of activities, for days without the phone (§13);
- the **utility caddy for Tend** (§3);
- a **textile or throw** for the Reading Nook or Garden Window feeling;
- a **mat** for Stretch / Move.

**[NON-COMMITMENT]** This is not an approved company strategy. It is a hypothesis worth validating (§9).

- optional World-themed packaging;
- small bundles built around one ritual (for example, a caddy, cloth and notebook for a "home reset" ritual);
- seasonal editions, following the World's seasons, with **no artificial scarcity, countdowns or "last chance" framing** ([Playbook Ch.4 §12](../playbook/04-product-decision-framework.md#12-things-thirty-will-probably-never-build));
- small World collections, coherent rather than collectible;
- physical objects informing *optional, user-chosen* visual personalization much later, only within [Signature Objects §8](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md#8-personalization--proposed-rule) and its reconciliation with [ADR-018](../product/adr/ADR-018-v1-world-art-scene-roles-and-daypart.md) ("no cosmetic editions") and [ADR-004](../product/adr/ADR-004-optional-data-never-required.md);
- gifting: giving the analog ritual to someone who doesn't use the app.

### 7.1 Hard boundaries

- **Buying a physical product must never unlock essential app functionality.** The Free product is complete ([PREMIUM_STRATEGY §1](../product/PREMIUM_STRATEGY.md#1-free-product-promise); [ADR-007](../product/adr/ADR-007-premium-never-blocks-the-core-loop.md)).
- **No paywall architecture** is proposed, implied or prepared.
- **Premium is not changed.** No physical purchase is tied to a Premium entitlement, trial, discount or upgrade path. Any such idea would need its own ADR against the Premium freeze.
- **No commerce inside the ritual.** There are no shop surfaces in the daily flow, no product prompts at Circle Closed, and no product links from inside a World ([SO §9.1](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md#91-principles)).
- **No coupling to behaviour.** Products are never tied to Circle counts, milestones or streaks ([SO §13](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md#13-anti-gamification)).
- **No data.** No purchase data is linked to in-app behaviour, and no product requires an account ([ADR-006](../product/adr/ADR-006-no-account-before-first-value.md)).

## 8. Commercial filter

THIRTY must eventually be commercially sustainable for KEYBRACE and its founder. Physical products could contribute to that, but only if they are chosen with discipline. This filter is a **lightweight framework for later**, when real supplier, sample and cost data exist.

### 8.1 Dimensions

| Dimension | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| **Brand fit** | Does it pass the [Golden Rule](../BRAND_BOOK.md#1-the-golden-rule): calmer, simpler, more human, or only bigger? |
| **Ritual fit** | Does it help someone do their thirty minutes, or only display the brand? |
| **Recognisability** | Is it recognisable as THIRTY without a large logo (§1)? |
| **Manufacturing complexity** | How hard is it to make well and consistently at small scale? |
| **MOQ risk** | How large is the minimum order relative to proven demand? |
| **Upfront capital** | How much money is committed before the first sale? |
| **Gross-margin potential** | After real unit cost, fulfilment and returns, is there a healthy margin at a price that still feels fair? |
| **Fulfilment complexity** | Can it be stored, packed and shipped without a heavy operation? |
| **Shipping cost sensitivity** | Does bulk or weight make shipping a large share of the price? |
| **Breakage / return risk** | How often will it arrive broken, disappoint, or come back? |
| **International scalability** | Do regulations, duties, certifications or carriers limit where it can be sold? |
| **Repeat-purchase potential** | Is there a natural reason to buy again (refills, new volumes, gifts) without manufactured urgency? |
| **Bundle potential** | Does it combine naturally with other ritual products? |
| **Differentiation** | Would a competitor's version be meaningfully different? |
| **Founder operational burden** | How much ongoing founder time does it take away from the app? |

### 8.2 How to use it later

- **No scores are assigned now.** Without real data, any score would be invented.
- When data exists, assess each candidate **qualitatively per dimension** (strong / acceptable / weak / unknown) and **record the evidence** behind each judgement.
- **Brand fit, ritual fit and founder burden are gates, not weights.** A product that fails one of them is not rescued by margin.
- **Unknowns are treated as risks**, not as neutral. A candidate with many unknowns goes back to validation (§9), not forward to production.
- Re-assess when circumstances change, for example when the business entity, merchant setup or app traction changes.

## 9. Possible validation sequence

This is a **low-risk sequence for later**, ordered by risk and capital, not by date.

**[NON-COMMITMENT]** No stage is scheduled, and **none of this is on the current pre-release critical path.**

**Stage 0 — No action until the fundamentals justify it**

- The app ships and proves itself first.
- *Prerequisite in the founder's own records.* The *Pre-release Vision & Delivery Tracker* records that a KEYBRACE legal and merchant structure must be in place before commercial sale, and that it is currently on hold. Physical sales would depend on that too, plus product-specific obligations such as product safety, consumer law, VAT and returns.
- Until then, at most this document and occasional sketches.

**Stage 1 — Concept validation (no inventory)**

- mockups and sample pages;
- preference testing with a small group;
- interest signals on a simple product page or waitlist, without taking payment unless a legal merchant structure exists;
- reactions to social content showing the World-to-object story.

**Stage 2 — Very small production or print-on-demand test**

- The **coloring book is the likeliest first candidate**, because sample pages and POD may allow a cheap, inventory-free test (§5.1). Quality must be confirmed with physical samples.

**Stage 3 — A simple physical product**

- the **Write notebook**, **ritual cards** or a similar paper-based product;
- small runs only, once Stage 2 evidence supports it.

**Stage 4 — Operationally more complex Signature Objects**

- textiles (throws), the **caddy**, the **mat**, the bottle;
- only with demand evidence, supplier samples, and a clear view of capital, MOQ and returns (§8).

Each stage needs its own evidence and its own explicit decision before the next one begins. **Skipping stages is the main risk this sequence exists to prevent.**

## 10. What not to do

These anti-patterns are explicitly recorded:

- **A generic logo T-shirt as the core strategy.**
- **Mass-producing many SKUs before validation.**
- **Forcing every World to have merch.** Quiet Trail and Still Lake are complete without it (§2.5–§2.6).
- **Turning artwork props into products just because they exist.** Presence in the art is not demand.
- **Products that only make sense with a large THIRTY logo.**
- **Letting physical commerce delay the app.**
- **Tying Premium entitlement to physical purchases prematurely**, or at all without an ADR against the Premium freeze.
- **Committing inventory capital before demand evidence.**
- **Cheap-looking AI-generated coloring pages.**
- **Generic stock mandalas with THIRTY branding.**
- **Changing artwork to make a prop more sellable** ([SO §10](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md#10-artwork-rule--proposed-rule-consistent-with-current-practice)).
- **Scarcity, drops, countdowns, collectible pressure or "complete the set"** ([Playbook Ch.4 §12](../playbook/04-product-decision-framework.md#12-things-thirty-will-probably-never-build); [SO §13](../design/THIRTY_SIGNATURE_OBJECTS.md#13-anti-gamification)).
- **Commerce inside the daily ritual**, such as shop prompts at Circle Closed or product links in a World.
- **Therapeutic or health-outcome claims** for any product.

## 13. Additional hypotheses

These follow directly from existing THIRTY material. They are **speculative** and are **not** a catalogue.

- **World art prints / postcards [HYPOTHESIS].** The approved Worlds are already finished watercolor-style scenes in three dayparts. A small set of prints of one World across morning, day and evening would be the most literal "came out of a World" product: no new design object, and LOW manufacturing complexity. Before any reproduction, it depends entirely on the artwork's commercial rights (§12, item 4). It must never become "buy the World": Worlds stay complete and free in the app ([World System §12](../worlds/WORLD_SYSTEM.md#12-free-and-premium)).
- **Activity ritual cards [HYPOTHESIS].** THIRTY's activities each carry a short, concrete first action in the app's activity catalogue. A small card deck of thirty-minute ritual prompts, illustrated with World vocabulary, would be an analog expression of the same idea: paper-based, LOW complexity, independent of the app. It must not reproduce the recommendation logic, imply personalised guidance, or make health claims.
- **Garden Window ritual set [HYPOTHESIS].** The `tend` Scene already composes basket, cloth and small tools as one arrangement [OBSERVED]. A "home reset" set built around the caddy (§3) follows the artwork's own grouping rather than an invented bundle.
- **Seasonal coloring volumes [HYPOTHESIS].** The World System already defines seasons ([§9](../worlds/WORLD_SYSTEM.md#9-seasons)). Seasonal coloring volumes would follow an existing THIRTY rhythm, without scarcity framing.
