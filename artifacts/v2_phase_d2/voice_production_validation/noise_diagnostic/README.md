## Verdict

**The noise is generated by ElevenLabs:** it is part of these two voices.
- **What it is:** a steady room tone, broadband and weighted towards the low end. It sits under every pause of every raw clip.
- **What mastering does:** the Guarded gain (+4.9 to +7.4 dB) raises the noise together with the voice. Mastering does not change the noise-to-speech ratio.
- **Not the cause:** the limiter and the AAC encode.
- **Changing the seed does not change it.**

**How the measurements were taken:**
- **Floor:** the 5th percentile of 20 ms frame levels within speech, with digital-silence padding excluded.
- **Loudness:** the mean integrated LUFS per clip.
- **Tools:** `tools/` here, plus the scratch analysis summarised below.

**What this means for listening:**
- **Level-matched to the approved voices,** Andre's noise sits about **20–30 dB higher** than theirs, and Lunaria's about **15–25 dB higher**.
- **Why it was less obvious before:** in the "as generated" files the noise is just as present, but 5–7 dB quieter, because the whole clip is.

### Where it enters, stage by stage

1. **Generated by ElevenLabs: YES, the root cause.**
   - **Present in every raw clip:**
     - Andre's floor is −62 to −66 dBFS in every clip.
     - Lunaria's floor is −64 to −68 dBFS.
     - Locke's raw pauses, by contrast, fall to digital silence (−91 dBFS).
   - **Steady:** the quiet-frame spread is 5.4 dB for Andre and 8.6 dB for Lunaria. There are no tonal peaks: it is not hum and not a codec tone.
   - **Spectrum of Andre's noise:** the largest share is in 63–250 Hz (−5 dB of the total) and in 31–63 Hz (−8.5 dB). It is a low, roomy rumble.
   - **Spectrum of Lunaria's noise:** broader, from 125 Hz to 8 kHz, with more hiss.
   - **The original raw head and tail** returned by ElevenLabs carry the same ambience: Andre −59 to −62 dBFS, Lunaria −59 to −69 dBFS.
2. **Revealed by gain: YES.** The Guarded gain raises the floor by exactly the gain: Andre −64.3 → −57.9, Lunaria −67.1 → −62.4. The ratio is unchanged (34.8 → 34.6 dB, 40.1 → 39.2 dB). That gain is needed to bring these voices near Locke and Heather.
3. **Introduced by limiting: NO.**
   - **Test:** an aligned limited-versus-unlimited comparison on each voice's most-limited Guarded clip.
   - **Result:** the limiter changes only 2.6 % (Andre) and 0.6 % (Lunaria) of the 20 ms frames, all of them on speech peaks.
   - **Change in the pauses:** −95 dBFS and −90 dBFS, which is 30–35 dB under the noise.
   - **Side finding, not audible:** `alimiter` adds a 5 ms look-ahead delay. On limited clips, `master2.limit` therefore trims the last 5 ms of the 40 ms trim margin. The production rule should compensate for it (`latency=1`).
4. **Introduced by the MP3 → AAC transcode: NO.**
   - **Test:** stage C (float) against stage D (decoded `.m4a`), sample-aligned.
   - **Pause floors:** C and D agree within 0.1 dB.
   - **Encode residual in pauses:** 24–31 dB below the noise floor.
   - **Whole-clip encode SNR:** 32–45 dB.
5. **Other contributing factor (perception, not a source):** each clip is trimmed with a hard cut and padded with digital silence. The room tone therefore switches on with each clip and off after it, which makes it read as noise rather than as natural room. Locke's and Heather's floors are too low for this to matter.

**Conclusion:** no safe post-processing removes this noise materially.
- **The gentle expander is the only candidate:** it is artefact-free by measurement but lowers the pauses by only about 3.5 dB, which is roughly the threshold of "just noticeable".
- **The noise under speech stays:** no processing that leaves the voice untouched can reach it.
- **The founder's ear decides** whether 3.5 dB is enough.

## Current audio pipeline

1. **Source:** ElevenLabs `eleven_multilingual_v2`, `mp3_44100_128`: MPEG-1 Layer III, 128 kbit/s CBR, 44.1 kHz, mono.
   - The best this plan allows: PCM 44.1 kHz needs Pro, and MP3 192 kbit/s needs Creator. Both were refused by the API on 2026-10-12.
2. **Decode:** ffmpeg to float32, 44.1 kHz, mono.
3. **Processing:**
   1. trim to speech (−50 dBFS envelope, 40 ms margin, hard cut);
   2. Guarded gain: an outlier correction into a ±2 LU window, then one voice gain;
   3. a 4× look-ahead peak limiter at −2 dBFS, only where peaks exceed it;
   4. digital-silence padding: 150 ms in; 700 or 400 ms out.
4. **Final encode:** ffmpeg native AAC-LC, 128 kbit/s, mono, 44.1 kHz, in `.m4a`, played by Android `MediaPlayer`.
5. **Lossy-to-lossy:** **YES** (MP3 → AAC). It is measured as not audible: the encode residual is 24–31 dB under the voices' own noise floor.

### Alternatives considered for avoiding the second lossy encode

| Route | Avoids the second encode? | Meets the app requirements? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| PCM 44.1 kHz source → one AAC encode | yes | yes | Needs the **Pro** plan. It would not remove this noise, which is in the voice itself. |
| Lower-rate PCM source (e.g. 24 kHz) → one AAC encode | yes | only after upsampling; 12 kHz bandwidth versus MP3's ~16 kHz | Not verified on this plan, and it trades codec loss for audible bandwidth loss. Not recommended. |
| Ship the ElevenLabs MP3 untouched | yes | no: no trim, gain or limiter, so the levels are uneven | Rejected: it is the loudness problem the founder already heard. |
| Lossless intermediate in the app (FLAC/WAV) | yes | yes; MediaPlayer plays FLAC | Size: 168 clips ≈ 10× the AAC size, for an unmeasurable gain. Not worth it. |

## Recommended production pipeline

- **Keep:** the current chain and the shipping format (one AAC-LC 128 kbit/s `.m4a`). The transcode is not the problem.
- **Two small, inaudible-risk refinements** for the production rule, applied to every voice:
  1. compensate the limiter's 5 ms look-ahead (`alimiter latency=1`);
  2. a 10 ms raised-cosine fade at each trim edge, so the room tone never starts or stops on a hard cut.
- **On applying them:** neither changes the approved Locke or Heather masters audibly. When the production pack is built, re-verify those masters by listening, because their hashes will change.
- **What decides Andre and Lunaria:** the source, not the pipeline. See Production readiness below.
